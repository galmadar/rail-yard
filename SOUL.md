# Rail Yard — what it is

_Draft for Gal to approve._

## What this toy is

You drive one little shunting engine around a goods yard, set the points, and
push and pull wagons until each one is standing on the road the job sheet asks
for. That is the whole idea: couple on, cut off, move them about. The fun is in
the weight of it. The engine is quick off the mark and stops hard, so you can
aim it. Roll gently into a wagon and it hooks on with a "coupled up"; come in
too fast and you get a thump. Cut a wagon off while you are moving and it keeps
rolling on its own. The puzzle is the order things end up in: whatever you
shove down a road first ends up deepest, and a loop or a triangle lets you turn
a train round.

## What it is not

- Not a timetable or railway-running game. No clocks, no schedules, no trains
  arriving to meet.
- Not a money or management game. No wages, no buying engines, no building
  your own yard.
- Not passengers. Goods wagons only, no stations, no people.
- Not a main-line driving game. You stay in the yard at shunting speeds; there
  is no route out to somewhere else.
- Not a crash game. Wrong points stop you, they don't throw you off the track,
  and a hard bump is a telling-off, not a wreck.
- Not a race. There is no timer and no score for being quick; a job is done
  when the wagons are in the right places.
